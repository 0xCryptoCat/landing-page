Offline progress and away time

Your farm keeps working after you close the app. Honey Inc offline earnings run for a base window of two hours, extended by one hour for every honey jar you hold.

The away time window

Idle game offline progress is usually capped, and Honey, Inc. is no exception — but the cap here is something you control rather than something fixed. Two numbers define it:

  • Base offline window: 2 hours. This is what every farm starts with.
  • +1 hour per honey jar. Each jar you hold adds another hour of stored offline time on top of the base.

So the window is additive and entirely legible. A farm with no jars banks two hours of away time; a farm with ten jars banks twelve. Time spent away beyond your window is simply not counted — the farm produced up to the cap and then stopped.

Read the table as a planning tool, not a target. The right window is the one that covers your longest normal gap between sessions. If you always open the game morning and evening, anything past about twelve hours is capacity you are not using.

Bees work at 80% while you are away

Offline production is not identical to watched production. While the app is closed, your bees work at 80% efficiency. The remaining fifth is the game's honest tax on not being there.

As a first approximation, that means eight hours away is worth roughly the honey of six and a half hours of watched play, and a full twelve-hour window is worth about nine and a half hours. It is a real discount, but it is a mild one — 80% is generous by the standards of the genre, and it is applied to production rate rather than to the reward at the end.

That last distinction matters, because the second number in this system is the one people miss: 100% of the honey produced offline is retained. There is no second haircut when you come back, no spoilage, no percentage skimmed off the collection. What your bees made at 80% rate, you keep in full. The only reduction in the entire offline path is that single efficiency figure.

The away-time summary

When you reopen the game after a gap, Honey, Inc. shows an away-time summary: what happened while you were gone, before you go back to running the farm. It is the natural moment to take stock — check what the window actually covered, and decide whether your storage situation matches how you are playing.

It is also the moment to notice a mismatch. If the summary keeps telling you the same story every morning, and you know you were away for eleven hours with a six-hour window, five of those hours produced nothing at all.

Why jars are the stat that decides your idle income

Here is the strategic core of this page, and it is worth being blunt about: jars are what extend your idle window, so storage is the lever that matters most for players who check in infrequently.

Most upgrade decisions in the game are about rate. Better hives and a bigger Bee Colony raise honey per bee; a better Shipping Depot and larger vehicles move more of it; the research tree multiplies both. All of that is valuable, and all of it is what players instinctively spend on first, because the numbers on screen move immediately.

But rate only pays out for as long as the clock is running. If you play in two short sessions a day with a ten-hour gap between them, a farm with a six-hour window converts your absence into six hours of income no matter how enormous the production rate is. Four hours of that gap are worth exactly nothing. Raising the window converts dead time into live time — and unlike a rate upgrade, it does so on the hours you were never going to be present for anyway.

The practical reading: production upgrades scale the hours you already bank, and jars decide how many hours you bank at all. Under-invest in the second and you are quietly running a smaller game than the one you paid for in upgrades.

The two playstyles pull in different directions. If you play in long sessions, rate is king and your window rarely fills — spend accordingly, and tap wasps while you are there, since those reward presence rather than absence. If you check in twice a day, the window is the first thing to look at.

Offline progress and the rest of the game

Away time interacts with almost every other system, so it is worth knowing where it sits.

Honey type. Offline production happens in whatever honey type your farm is currently on, and the ten types range in base value from 2 for Basic Honey up to 18,000,000 for Temporal Honey. The same number of offline hours is worth wildly different amounts depending on where you are in that ladder — which is the strongest argument for making sure your window is generous before a long absence rather than after.

Farm value. Everything produced offline feeds the same farm value progression that gates honey type unlocks and Scale of Production tiers. Idle hours are not a lesser class of progress; they count identically.

Reincarnation. A reset trades your farm for Royal Jellies, each worth a permanent +1.2% earnings bonus up to a +25% cap. That bonus applies to a farm that is largely running while you are not watching it, which is a quiet argument for pushing reincarnation even if you play casually.

What still needs you. Away time covers production. It does not cover the parts of the game that require a tap: wasps only pay when someone is there to catch them, and boosts, crates, mail and daily rewards are all collected in-session. A well-tuned window means your farm is never idle in the wasteful sense — but the game still has things worth coming back for.
