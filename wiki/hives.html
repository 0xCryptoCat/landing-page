Hives and Hive Tiers

Four slots, nine tiers, and a price curve that multiplies by roughly six at every single step. This page explains how hives work in Honey, Inc. and what each hive upgrade actually costs.

Hives are where your bees come from. A Honey, Inc. farm holds a maximum of four hive slots, and each slot can be filled with a hive from one of nine hive tiers. That is the entire structure — there is no fifth slot to unlock later and no hidden tenth tier. What changes over a run is which tier occupies each of the four slots, and how much the next one costs.

The important thing to understand before spending anything is that slots are priced individually, not as a set. Buying a hive does not make the next one cheaper or the same price. It makes it several times more expensive, and that escalation runs across tiers as well as within them.

The tier names

The first three tiers are Base Hive, Improved Hive and Advanced Hive, in that order. Tiers four through nine exist, but this wiki does not list their names — we would rather leave a gap than print a guess, so they are referred to here simply as the later tiers. If you have seen them in game and want them added, the contact link in the footer is real.

Slot costs escalate individually

This is the part players most often get wrong. Each additional slot at a given tier costs substantially more than the previous slot at that same tier. Four Base Hives do not cost four times one Base Hive — the first is free, and the fourth costs 3,600.

Slot Base Hive (tier 1) Improved Hive (tier 2)
Slot 1021,600
Slot 2100130,000
Slot 3600778,000
Slot 43,6004,670,000
All four slots4,3005,599,600

Read across the two columns and the shape becomes obvious. Filling every Base Hive slot costs 4,300 cash in total — pocket change within the first session. Filling every Improved Hive slot costs 5,599,600, roughly 1,300 times as much. And that is only the jump from tier 1 to tier 2, with seven tiers still above it.

The ×6 rule. Every step in the numbers above is almost exactly six times the previous one: 100 to 600, 600 to 3,600, 3,600 to 21,600, then 21,600 to 130,000 to 778,000 to 4,670,000. Costs beyond the Improved Hive are not listed on this wiki, so treat ×6 as an observation about the figures above rather than a promise about tiers 3 to 9.

Widen or deepen?

With four slots and nine tiers you are always choosing between two moves: add another hive at your current tier, or upgrade to the tier above. The costs make the answer clearer than it first looks.

The fourth Base Hive slot costs 3,600. The first Improved Hive costs 21,600 — six times more. So filling out your current tier is essentially always the cheaper action, and the price gap between "one more slot here" and "one slot at the next tier" stays at that same factor of six through every number we can verify. Early on, when cash is the binding constraint, filling all four slots at your current tier before pushing upward is the low-risk path.

The counter-argument is that money in this game grows exponentially. A cost that looks impossible now becomes trivial two farm value tiers later, and there is no refund for having bought the cheap thing first. Buying a fourth Base Hive for 3,600 when your income is about to make 21,600 routine is not a mistake so much as a rounding error — but it is worth noticing rather than doing on autopilot.

How hives feed bee spawning

Hives spawn bees; the bees fly out, collect, and return. More hives and better hives mean more spawning power pushing against your bee pool.

That pool is capped separately. Bee capacity starts at 50 and upgrades through five levels to 500, with refill rate running from 1 to 5.4 bees per second on its own five-level track. Hives are the source, capacity is the ceiling, and the two only help you when they are roughly in balance. If your bee pool sits permanently full, another hive slot buys you nothing until you raise capacity. If the pool is permanently empty, capacity is the wrong purchase and the hives are the constraint. Diagnosing which of the two you are looking at takes about ten seconds of watching the farm and saves a lot of cash.

What else raises hive output

Slots and tiers are the direct levers, but they are not the only ones. The regular research tree runs to thirteen tiers and 57 upgrade groups, and a large share of them are named after hives: Comfortable Hives, Better Hive Nurseries, Hive Expansion Project, Internal Hive Systems, Climate Controlled Hives, Industrial Hive Automation, AI Hive Management, Hive Mind Networking and Wormhole Hive Stabilization are all real group names in the tree.

Epic research adds 18 more upgrades bought with Honeycombs, several of them hive- and nursery-facing — Rapid Hive Activation, Epic Nursery Mastery, Epic Internal Nurseries, Nursery Redistribution, Nursery Hibernation and Epic Luxury Habitats. Epic research persists through reincarnation, which makes it the most durable place to put hard currency. On the temporary side, boosts include Hive Frenzy, Auto-Hive Spawn, Hive Prism and Legendary Hive Prism.

Hives across resets

Reincarnation resets the farm in exchange for Royal Jellies. It requires reaching at least honey tier 4, Acacia Honey, and a minimum of 25,000,000 earnings in the run. Your epic research and Honeycombs survive; the farm — hives included — does not. Each Royal Jelly is worth a permanent +1.2% earnings bonus up to a +25% cap, which is covered on the Royal Jelly page.

Switching honey type also starts a fresh farm at the new type's base value. Both events mean the hive money you spend in a run is spent for that run. Given the ×6 curve, that is an argument for not agonizing over the last slot at a tier you are about to leave behind.

A practical order. Fill your four slots at the current tier while they are cheap, keep bee capacity and refill roughly level with your spawning power, and only then look at the next tier. If honey is piling up unshipped instead, the constraint is downstream — check Storage and the Shipping Depot on the buildings page.

Where hives sit in the wider economy

Hives sit at the very front of the chain. Bees leave the hives, the Bee Colony sets what each bee is worth, Storage holds the honey, and the Shipping Depot and its vehicles convert it into cash. A hive upgrade only shows up in your income if every stage behind it can keep up, which is why hive spending and building spending should move together rather than in long separate bursts.
