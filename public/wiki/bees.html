Bees and Bee Capacity

Everything Honey, Inc. produces starts with a bee leaving a hive. This page covers bee capacity, refill rate, all ten upgrade levels with their real costs, and how to split your money between the two tracks.

In Honey, Inc. bees are the production unit. They are spawned from the hives on your farm, they fly out to collect, and they return. Every other system in this bee farm game either raises how much a single bee brings home or speeds up what happens to the honey afterwards — so if you are trying to work out why your income has stalled, the bees are usually the right place to start looking.

Two numbers control the flow. Bee capacity is how many bees your farm can hold at once. Refill rate is how quickly that pool tops itself back up, measured in bees per second. It helps to picture a bucket and a tap: capacity is the size of the bucket, refill rate is how fast the tap runs. Both are upgradeable, both have exactly five levels, and they cost very different amounts.

Bee capacity: 50 to 500

A new farm starts with a capacity of 50 bees. Five capacity levels take that to 500, doubling at the first step and then climbing more gently: 50, 100, 200, 350, 500. The first level is what you already own, so there are four purchases to make, and they get sharply more expensive — every step costs six times the one before it.

Refill rate: 1 to 5.4 bees per second

Refill rate also has five levels, starting at 1 bee per second and finishing at 5.4 per second. The intermediate steps are 1.6, 2.4 and 3.6. Notice that the whole track only multiplies your refill speed by 5.4×, while the capacity track multiplies the pool by 10×. That mismatch is the single most important thing to understand about bee upgrades, and we come back to it below.

Two patterns worth memorizing. Refill rate costs exactly double capacity at every matching level, and every step on both tracks is six times the previous one. Maxing both tracks costs 932,400 cash in total — two thirds of that on refill alone.

Capacity or refill rate first?

Because capacity is half the price at every level, it is tempting to buy the whole capacity track and leave refill alone. The arithmetic argues against it. Divide capacity by refill rate and you get the time it takes to fill an empty pool from scratch, assuming you keep both tracks at the same level:

  • Level 1 — 50 bees at 1.0/sec: about 50 seconds
  • Level 2 — 100 bees at 1.6/sec: about 63 seconds
  • Level 3 — 200 bees at 2.4/sec: about 83 seconds
  • Level 4 — 350 bees at 3.6/sec: about 97 seconds
  • Level 5 — 500 bees at 5.4/sec: about 93 seconds

Refill time creeps upward as you climb, because the pool grows faster than the tap does. Now do the same sum for a lopsided farm: a maxed 500-bee capacity still sitting on the starting 1 bee per second takes over eight minutes to refill. You paid 259,200 for a pool you cannot keep full, and for most of those eight minutes the farm is running on a fraction of the bees it could have.

The reverse mistake is cheaper but just as real. Maxed refill with a 50-bee pool fills in under ten seconds and then simply stops, because there is nowhere for the extra bees to go. Capacity sets the ceiling of a wave; refill rate sets how often you get one. Keeping the two tracks within a level of each other is the reading the numbers support — buy a capacity level, then buy the refill level that matches it, then move on.

Where this money could go instead. 932,400 cash is a serious sum in the early game. Compare it against a fourth hive slot or the next tier of the Bee Colony before you commit — bee upgrades raise the number of workers, but the Colony raises what each one is worth.

Where bees come from

Bees are spawned by hives, and a farm holds a maximum of four hive slots across nine hive tiers. Hives are the source; capacity is the ceiling. That distinction matters when you are diagnosing a slow farm — if your pool is permanently full, more spawning power will not help you, and if it is permanently empty, more capacity will not either.

What a bee is worth once it flies is set elsewhere. The Bee Colony building starts at a base honey rate of 0.5 per bee and runs to twelve levels, with an efficiency multiplier that reaches 133.4× at maximum. The honey those bees deliver then passes through Storage and the Shipping Depot before it becomes cash, so a farm can be bee-rich and still bottlenecked further down the line. The buildings page covers all four in detail, and vehicles covers the fleet that actually carries the honey out.

Bees while you are away

Bees keep working after you close the app, at 80% efficiency. The base offline window is two hours, and each honey jar adds another hour of stored offline time. All of the honey produced while you are gone is retained — none of it is lost — and the game shows an away-time summary when you come back. Full details are on the offline progress page.

Upgrades beyond the two tracks

Capacity and refill are not the end of bee scaling. The regular research tree spans thirteen tiers and 57 distinct upgrade groups, several of which are named after bee and hive behavior — Active Forager Bonus, Better Hive Nurseries, Synchronized Foraging, Formation Flying Training, Queen Bee Optimization and Super Pollen Diet are all real group names. The research page lists them properly.

There are also 18 epic research upgrades bought with Honeycombs, including Epic Flight Efficiency, Epic Swarm Multiplier and Epic Nursery Mastery. Epic research persists through reincarnation, which makes it the one category of bee investment you never have to buy twice. Temporary multipliers come from boosts such as Hive Frenzy, Buzz Beacon and Auto-Hive Spawn, and permanent earnings come from Royal Jellies — each one grants +1.2%, capped at +25% total. See Royal Jelly for that math.

Bees and long-term progression

Progress in Honey, Inc. is measured by farm value, not by a player level — there is no XP. Farm value is what gates the ten honey types, and honey type is what multiplies the value of every jar your bees fill. A larger, faster-refilling bee pool raises farm value in its own right and shortens the climb to the next honey tier, which is where the real income jumps live.

One planning note: reincarnation resets the farm, and both bee tracks go with it. That is not a reason to skip them — you need the throughput to reach the required honey tier in the first place — but it is a reason to buy them in the order the numbers support rather than maxing one track for the sake of a full bar.
