Honey, Inc. buildings

The four structures on your farm — Bee Colony, Honey Extractor, Shipping Depot and Research Lab — with their real level tracks, their ceilings, and honest advice on which one to upgrade next.

Honey, Inc. buildings are the four permanent structures that decide how much honey your farm makes, how much of it you can hold, how quickly it turns into cash, and how much your research is worth. Every Bee Colony upgrade, every honey extractor level and every depot level is bought outright with cash and applies straight away — there is nothing to research or wait for first. This page lists what each building actually scales, the numbers behind the levels, and the reasoning behind a sensible upgrade order.

The four buildings form a chain. Bees produce honey, the extractor stores and processes it, the depot ships it for money, and the lab multiplies what your research contributes to all of it. Because it is a chain, the building worth upgrading is almost always whichever one is currently holding the others up — not whichever one has the biggest number attached to it.

Bee Colony

The Bee Colony sets honey produced per bee. The base rate is 0.5 honey per bee, and the building has twelve levels. Because the effect is per bee, it multiplies against however many bees you are running, which makes it the one building that interacts directly with the bee capacity and refill upgrades and with how many hives you have placed. A colony upgrade on a farm with 50 bees and the same upgrade on a farm with 500 bees are not the same purchase.

The remaining six levels continue the same climb. At maximum level the colony's efficiency multiplier reaches 133.4× the starting rate. The "multiple of base" column above is simply each level's rate divided by the 0.5 starting rate, included so the shape of the curve is visible: growth is steady rather than explosive, which is why colony levels alone rarely fix a stalled farm.

Storage — the Honey Extractor

Storage starts at a capacity of 100 and runs to twelve levels, where the capacity multiplier reaches 4,095×. That is by far the largest ceiling of the four buildings, and it is the reason storage is easy to misjudge: a big capacity number does not create honey, it only stops you from running out of room for it. Capacity is a ceiling, not a rate.

Shipping Depot

The Shipping Depot converts stored honey into cash. Its base shipping rate is 1 and it has twelve levels, with the shipping multiplier reaching 243.8× at the top. It also houses your fleet: all twelve vehicles, from the Base Cart carrying 17 per trip up to the Hexaloop Tanker at 16,666,667, are unlocked through depot level rather than bought on their own screen.

That makes the depot the only building with two distinct payoffs. The multiplier improves shipping directly, and the level unlock puts a larger vehicle on the road. If honey is piling up in storage faster than it leaves, the depot — not the colony — is the building that is behind.

Research Lab

The Research Lab is the odd one out: six levels rather than twelve, and it produces nothing by itself. It applies a multiplier to research, running 1 → 1.6 → 2.5 → 4 → 6.5 → 10×. Each level has an unlock threshold you must reach before it becomes available.

Because the lab multiplies research rather than production, its value depends entirely on how much research you have bought. The regular research tree runs to 13 tiers and 57 distinct upgrade groups, so a player deep into that tree gets far more out of a lab level than someone who has barely started it. The 18 epic research upgrades are a separate track bought with Honeycombs, and they survive reincarnation.

Which building should you upgrade first

The honest answer is that the four ceilings are not comparable. Storage tops out at 4,095× and the colony at 133.4×, but those multiply completely different quantities — capacity against production — so picking the biggest number is the wrong instinct. What works is watching where the farm stalls:

  • Storage sits full for long stretches. Production is outrunning your ability to hold or move honey. Upgrade the extractor, the depot, or both.
  • Storage sits near empty and the depot has nothing to move. The bottleneck is upstream. Buy Bee Colony levels, more bee capacity, or another hive.
  • Both look healthy but income is flat. This is when the Research Lab and the research tree earn their keep, because they scale the whole chain rather than one link in it.

Early on, keeping the colony, extractor and depot roughly in step is more effective than pushing any one of them far ahead. The chain only runs as fast as its slowest link, and a maxed extractor on a farm with 50 bees is storage you will never fill.

Buildings reset, some progress does not. Building levels are part of the farm, so reincarnation clears them in exchange for Royal Jellies, while epic research and Honeycombs carry over. Switching to a new entry in the honey type list also starts a fresh farm at that type's base value — a mid-game reset rather than a straight upgrade.

How buildings fit the wider farm

Building levels are one of the main things that move your farm value, which is the measure the game uses in place of a player level, and which gates the honey types you can unlock. They also interact with the temporary boosts you buy or win, and with the cash and Honeycombs you pick up from wasps flying across the farm.
